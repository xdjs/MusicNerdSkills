# Visual system

## Style and typography

Use the pilot as a visual reference: imperfect but intentional, not random jitter. Pink #FF75D8, near-black field, off-white paper, rough edges and pink tape. Maintain visible paper thickness and organic variation; avoid near-invisible sections, fragile slivers and distracting frame collisions. Do not substitute a generic glasses logo or redraw an inaccurate version.

Use verified project fonts/assets. Inter is Music Nerd's intended brand type direction; some pilot end-card scripts used Arial as an implementation fallback. Do not silently treat that fallback as a new brand standard or replace approved raster artwork. Verify availability, compare the actual approved appearance and disclose a necessary substitution. Avoid tiny text that works only on a desktop.

The pilot review is 1280×720 at 24fps. Express layout as normalized coordinates for other sizes and inspect the actual output. Do not upscale that review and claim a new 1080p source-quality master. Rebuild from original cameras for a higher-resolution master.

## Shared layout

- Persistent mark remains fixed at top left; move/inset the stage, not the brand mark, when camera layout changes.
- Pilot reference: logo x48/y28 at 1280×720, approximately 189×25px; this was a 10% enlargement over its earlier version. Remove the tiny persistent “Podcast by Music Nerd” line; title/end cards can carry the full identity.
- Two-person and screen-share stages were inset 7.5% with eased geometry to align with the header gutter. This is a matching reference, not an unquestionable percentage for all screen content. Verify the shared app remains legible, cursor/microtext is not the only carrier of meaning, and no border touches another border.
- Keep a clean dark buffer below the mark. Do not add another badge, tape patch, dividing line or quote simply to fill space.
- Cold-open lockup matches the body. Remove redundant glasses above the descriptive tagline; keep approved top-left show identity.
- Title and ending may use a larger centered mark. Preserve exact tagline when used: “Conversations with creatives at the intersection of music & tech”.

## Names, illustrations and inserts

Use the same torn-paper/tape lower-third template for Pete and guests, with text-driven width, matching type scale, animation, padding and clearance from the camera border. Do not revive the older plain white host strip with pink slashes. Pilot reference: roughly 36px right and 40px bottom clearance within its camera at 720p; adapt to camera geometry and text length rather than screen coordinates.

Check names, pronouns/titles and handles against current sources. A former artist name is not automatically the current name. Fit two-line names by deliberate layout, not arbitrarily small type.

Show collaborators with sourced portraits, hand-drawn arrows/maps and concise identity labels. Explain relationships actually mentioned; do not invent documentary imagery or suggest one artist endorsed another. SuperPhone/Ryan Leslie and collaborator sequences worked because they supplied context. Repeated generic quote cards did not.

Keep drawings recognizable at viewing scale (e.g. hood opening, long sleeves, cuffs, kangaroo pocket for a hoodie). Enlarge important illustrations and reach the readable pose promptly. Let compositions settle rather than keeping everything moving. Center titles over their related object with breathing room; the pilot “LIVE MUSIC” text was corrected above a phone for this reason.

Avoid extra quote lines over screen share: the audience already watches the app and two people. Keep occasional purposeful emphasis, not a repeated shrink-camera/read-text/expand-camera loop. Do not switch briefly to an unframed camera only to reapply the overlay.

## Preview and consistency check

Compare cold open, single speaker, two-speaker, screen-share, illustration/quote, title and end card. Check entry/exit frames, not just settled poses. Look at negative space, baseline alignment, safe margins, rough-border thickness, nameplate overlap and phone-size readability. Use actual source motion, not unrelated reaction shots. If clips are vertical later, recompose deliberately; do not blindly center-crop the landscape two-person layout.

Caption tracks and burned-in graphics must not overlap. If captions are requested, correct ASR speaker names/proper nouns, use readable contrast, and retain optional caption files rather than assuming every full episode needs burned-in captions. Keep fast flashes/glitch movement restrained for readability and avoid unnecessary strobing.
